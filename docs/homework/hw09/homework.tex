\courseheader{9}{Tool-camera visual servoing}
\textbf{Schedule:} week 9. You will steer the project's four-link arm with
the camera mounted on its tool until an object sits under the tool, then
pick it up. Feedback comes from the image only: the controller never knows
where the object is in the world. Units are meters, seconds, radians, and
pixels.

\textbf{Camera.} The tool camera looks straight down from $0.05$ m above
the tool, offset $-0.06$ m along the tool's $x$ axis: camera $x$ = tool $x$,
camera $y$ = $-$tool $y$, camera $z$ points down. Focal length $f=180$ px;
images are $320\times240$ with $(u,v)=(160,120)$ on the optical axis ($u$
right, $v$ down). The tool is $0.25$ m above the belt while the pickup
actuator is up.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Project points by hand.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A point is at $p_c=[0.11,\ -0.025,\ 0.30]^T$ m in the camera frame.
Using $u=f\,x_c/z_c+160$ and $v=f\,y_c/z_c+120$, find its pixel.
\item Show that $[0.22,\ -0.05,\ 0.60]^T$ lands on the same pixel. Why can
one image not give a point's depth? Why is the depth $Z$ of a belt point
known in this task, and what is it?
\item The \emph{desired pixel} $s^*$ is where a belt point directly below the
tool appears. Find $s^*$ and explain why it is not the image center.
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{The constant-depth image Jacobian.}
Let the camera translate parallel to the belt with velocity
$v_c=[v_x,v_y]^T$ in its own frame, without rotating.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a fixed belt point at constant depth $Z$, show that
$\dot u=-(f/Z)\,v_x$ and $\dot v=-(f/Z)\,v_y$, so $\dot s=L\,v_c$ with
$L=-(f/Z)I_2$.
\item With error $e=s-s^*$, find the $v_c$ that makes $\dot e=-\lambda e$
for a gain $\lambda>0$. What are the units of $\lambda$ and of $Z/f$?
\item The tool's heading is $\psi$. Write the camera's $x$ and $y$ axes in
the world and show that the world velocity is
\[
v_w=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi&\sin\psi\\ \sin\psi&-\cos\psi\end{bmatrix}v_c .
\]
\item The object of problem 1(a) is seen at $s=(226,105)$; $\lambda=2$
s$^{-1}$ and $\psi=\pi/2$. Compute $e$, $v_c$, and $v_w$. Using problem 1,
find the object's position relative to the tool in the world and show
that here $v_w=\lambda\,(p_{\rm object}-p_{\rm tool})$, although the
controller never computed either position.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[start=3]
\item \textbf{Implement the IBVS step.}
In \path{homework/hw09/ibvs_controller.py}, complete:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{ibvs\_velocity(pixel, target\_pixel, depth, focal, yaw, gain)}.
It is your problem 2 written as code; check it against 2(d).
\item \texttt{IBVSController.joint\_rates(pixel, jacobian, yaw)}: return
zero joint rates if the target is lost (\texttt{pixel is None}); otherwise
limit the speed of \texttt{ibvs\_velocity}'s result to \texttt{MAX\_SPEED} and
solve for joint rates with your HW06 \texttt{damped\_least\_squares}, using
all three rows of \texttt{jacob\_planar} and a desired heading rate of zero.
Why does problem 2 need the heading held? How many joints are left over?
\end{enumerate}
The provided \texttt{step()} finds the object with a color threshold, calls
your method, and requests a pick once the error stays under 4 px while the
tool is nearly still. It is the project's \texttt{Controller} class: the
command it returns is applied one period (0.02 s) later.

\item \textbf{Run and examine the loop.}
\begin{lstlisting}
python -m homework.hw09.hw09_ibvs --save tmp/hw09.png
python -m homework.hw09.hw09_ibvs --seed 2
\end{lstlisting}
The tool camera is blank (a covered lens) from 0.30 to 0.70 s.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For seeds 1 and 2, report the first and last pixel error, the pick
time, and whether the pickup succeeded. Estimate how long the error takes to
fall by a factor of $e\approx2.7$; compare with $1/\lambda$ and give one
reason they differ.
\item Confirm your commands were zero while the camera was blank. Why does
the error keep shrinking just after the camera goes blank? Why would reusing
the last measurement be risky with a moving object?
\item List what the controller took from a model ($f$, $Z$, $s^*$, $\psi$,
the Jacobian) and what it took from the image. In lecture, a controller that
measured the object with the fixed camera and moved the modeled tool there
missed when the arm's base was modeled 3 cm away from its true position;
IBVS still picked. Use your list to explain why.
\item In the project the objects move at about $0.06$ m/s. If you add
nothing to this law, the tool lags so that $\lambda(Z/f)\|e\|$ matches the
object's speed. Estimate that lag in pixels and meters for $\lambda=2$
s$^{-1}$. What term would you add to remove it?
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Project connection:} your \texttt{IBVSController} is a project
\texttt{Controller}. The project adds the fixed camera to choose and time
objects, the moving belt, and the trip to the bin.
